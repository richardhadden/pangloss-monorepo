# Report: step 10.5g, the worker measured at size (builder, 2026-11-24)

Written as the work goes; the figures are filled in as each run ends.

## Choices

1. **No new flag: `declare(suggested=True)` as it is.** A new flag was written first, and found not needed: a
   field that takes `IdentificationByToolchain[...]` and `Identification[...]` takes the classes below them, so
   `suggested=True` already lets a participant (in its place and in an `InRole`) be the worker's
   `IdentificationByToolchainFromText` and its `confirmed_as`, `IdentificationFromText`. So `declare` is unchanged
   (its docstring now says so) and every test of `suggested` declares what it did. A pure test holds it
   (`pangloss-reconcile/tests/test_reconcile_planted_worker_models.py`: both classes in a role and beside one, a
   suggestion refused as `carried_out_by`, none without `suggested`). The world is unchanged: a mention named in a
   participant's `InRole` sits where a suggestion goes (the shape "alternatives"); one named as `carried_out_by` (an
   activity with nobody else) is not.
2. **The world is not loaded again**: the containers hold the dated world of 10.5c and 11.7 (32,137 people, 6,427
   mentions; 38,566 `Person` on Memgraph and 38,564 on Neo4j, the two more being 8.6's written people; 96,715 and
   96,713 factoids). Their factoids have no `published` property, so with review installed they count as published
   (`fragments.published`: what is not marked is): the worker leaves them, `mark_stored` marks none of them, and only
   what the command pushes is unpublished.
3. **The models of the command** (`planted.py rebuild --worker-models`, and `worker`): the apps of the earlier
   commands, `pangloss_review` and `pangloss_reconcile`; `declare(dated=True, stand_in=True, reviewed=True,
   suggested=True)`. The rebuild is run with the same apps and models, or the signatures would differ.
4. **Saved as an upload would save them**: `pangloss_models.bulk_upload.upload(..., concurrency=8)` (what `load`
   writes its records with, here not in a bulk load's analytical mode), marked by **`RECONCILE_SUGGEST_ON_SAVE`**
   (upload says no instruction), which the command turns on and off on the settings between pushes.
5. **"Someone new"** as 10.5c's `suggest` has it: the mention's own `Person` (each mention of the planted world is a
   person of its own too, of the same name) and every other mention's are left out of what the worker is answered,
   and of every second mention the person it is as well, by a wrapper of `candidates.candidates_of` in the command's
   process: `best` filtered, `candidates` its first five not under the least score, as `suggest` filtered them.
   Nothing is deleted.
6. **The waves.** Three of `--push` (2,000) mentions each, from the planted world's mentions in their order (which
   is random): the first `[0, 2000)`, the second `[2000, 4000)`, the third `[4000, 6000)`; then 200 for 6a and 200
   for a fourth, small wave of 6d. The first is pushed marked (the push timed with the marking), the second right
   after it **not marked** (the same push without it: other mentions, the same size, the same database), then the
   vectors worker run to its end.
7. **The two waves compared, the cheaper honest way**: the second wave is stored, unmarked, before the worker does
   the first; it is **asked as the worker asks it** (`rewriting.read_head`, `stored.plan_of_view`,
   `candidates_of(..., see_unpublished=True, limit=DISPLAYED, only=...)`, `suggest.decide_for`; nothing written)
   before the first wave's suggestions exist; then the worker does the first wave; then `mark_stored` (6b) marks the
   second, and the worker does it: what it was answered and decided is recorded by the same wrappers, by stand-in id.
   So the only change between the two askings is the first wave's suggestions written (with the vectors' refresh
   they cause), which is what "an unconfirmed suggestion is no evidence" is about; the scores are compared part by
   part, so that a change of the graph part's (the documents rewritten move their neighbours' graph vectors) is
   told from one of `shared` or the name.
8. **6c** on the second wave: every second head's update is made without `still_mine` (the wrapper gives the update a
   `before_write` that does nothing), the other half with it timed alone. A single worker, so nothing is at risk.
9. **6d on a fourth wave of its own** (200 mentions), not on the second: a judge configured changes the digest of
   "tried", so the first wave's heads that `mark_stored` marks again (those whose stand-in was left) would all be
   asked again under it. The judge is `async`, in the script (`RECONCILE_SUGGEST_WITH = "__main__:unchanged_judge"`):
   the worker's own cost of asking one, not a thread's.
10. **The server's workers** (3): `pangloss_api.background.Supervisor` with the two `BackgroundTask`s the apps'
    `configure_app` add (`vectors`, `pangloss_vectors.worker.worker.run`; `reconcile`,
    `pangloss_reconcile.suggesting.worker.run`), started in the command's loop as the server starts them, then the
    third wave pushed in the same loop (marked on save); the writes wake both through the hooks as in the server. No
    HTTP and no lifespan (the planted database has no migration records: the API would not start).
11. **The marks a bulk load left**: the planted databases held 137,361 marks of `criteria` and 96,715 of `derive`
    (Neo4j 137,357 and 96,713), for workers never run here. `PGPending` is found by its label and the kind is not
    indexed, so every claim and count of the reconcile and vectors workers scans them. In a server those workers
    would have done them: they are deleted before the first push, and what a count cost with them is printed.
12. **Structure**: the indexes and constraints of the migrations of review (0001, 0003), the library (0005) and
    reconcile (0001 to 0003) made on the planted databases, which were bulk-loaded and never migrated.
13. **Timings and other test runs**: before each timed phase the command waits while `pgrep -f bin/pytest` finds
    one; each 30 s line says if one is going, and a phase during which one went is marked "not clean".
14. **A dry run first** on `:7706`, after the rebuild, with `--push 5 --offset 6400 --sample 5` (mentions the real
    run does not use): 20 factoids more in the world, said here.

**Pushes, Memgraph** (13:50, no test run): the first wave, marked, 2,000 in 48.3 s (41.4 a second with 8 writers; a
save 168 ms at the median, 90% 321 ms, most 1,041 ms); the second, not marked, 2,000 in 51.1 s (39.1 a second; 165

mentions sit in a participant's place (an activity of the world with nobody but the mention is rare enough that
none of these 4,000 has one): no stand-in was in `carried_out_by`.

**FOUND: the vectors worker's default batch fails at this size on Memgraph.** Its first batch (200 of the pushed
factoids) is one read transaction of the graph part (`reading.run`), which outlasted Memgraph's transaction timeout:
"read transaction reading.<locals>.run took 1200988 ms (slow)", retried by the driver, then
`TransientError: Transaction was asked to abort because of transaction timeout`, and `run_pending` raised. The dry
run had measured about 3.4 s a head for batches of 1 to 10 (a hearing names a family and two guild members; a guild
is a hub), so 200 heads are about 11 minutes in one transaction. In a server the worker halves its batch after
each failure down to 10, each failure costing a timeout (and the driver's retry). Not mended. From here the command
sets the vectors worker's batch to 50 (`--vectors-batch`), in the server's run too, and says so.

**The plan changed with it** (the time of the vectors worker at about 18 heads a minute decides the length of every
phase): waves 1 and 2 stay at 2,000 (already written); `mark_stored` runs before the reconcile worker, so that one
drain does the first wave and then the second (the oldest marks first: the whole first wave is written before the
second is begun, which the command checks), and the vectors worker runs once after it, not after each wave; of the
second wave every second head (1,000) is asked beforehand for the comparison; the server's wave is 1,000; the
judge's 100.

**The vectors worker on the two pushes, Memgraph**: batches of 50 took 166 to 179 s each (each logged as a slow read
of `reading.run`): **about 3.4 s a head, 17 to 18 heads a minute**, so 4,000 new factoids are about 3.8 hours of the
vectors worker before the reconcile worker may claim the last of them. (The run was stopped once by my shell's time
limit in this phase, at 14:35, and started again detached at 14:37; the batches done stay done, so the phase's own
time printed covers only what was left.)
