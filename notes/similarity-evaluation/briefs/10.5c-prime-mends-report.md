# 10.5c' mends report (builder): choices as they were made

Written as the work goes; the summary is at the end.

## Choices

1. **A judge's own `CancelledError`** (point 1) is caught in `judging.call`: while the worker's task is not being
   cancelled (`asyncio.current_task().cancelling() == 0`) it becomes `judging.JudgeCancelled` (an `Exception`),
   which `judged` makes a failure of the judge for that mention ("it cancelled itself (CancelledError) ..."). A real
   cancellation goes out as it is. `SystemExit`/`KeyboardInterrupt` are not caught anywhere new.
2. **The judge's threads** (point 2) are `judging.Threads`, the worker's own `ThreadPoolExecutor` of
   `judging.JUDGE_THREADS = 4` (a constant, not a setting: a judge that needs more at once is better `async`), made
   when first needed, named `pangloss-reconcile-judge-*`. `busy` counts the threads still running a judge (a lock;
   released when the judge returns, or when a queued item is cancelled before it ran). When `busy` is at the most,
   `run` raises `judging.Busy` at once ("the judge's threads are all still busy ..."), a failure of the judge for
   that mention, logged as the others. The sync judge runs in a **copy of the caller's context**
   (`contextvars.copy_context()`; `loop.run_in_executor` would not copy it, `asyncio.to_thread` did), so it sees the
   toolchain's user (point 6). `shutdown()` (`wait=False`) is called in `Worker.run`'s `finally` and in the module's
   `drain()`'s `finally` (the tests' and scripts' worker stops there); a thread still busy when the pool is let go
   counts against the next pool, so a hanging judge holds at most 4 threads in all, whatever restarts. `Judge`
   carries the worker's `threads`; `judging.call` takes `threads=` (required: there is no default pool).
3. **A judge that swallows the cancellation of its timeout** and returns: `call` now says `TimedOut` (checked by
   `limit.expired()` after the block), its answer not used. A small addition beside point 1.
4. **Huge weights** (point 3): `_weight` makes the float inside a `try` (an int too large is the bad weight
   message); the weights are divided by the largest before `percentages` (alternatives shape only; the one shape
   takes the heaviest anyway).
5. **The trial** (point 4) runs, in this order: the class against the worker's own `takes` (its message first);
   `prepare_rewrite` with the replacement **of a known id**, `to_db_model`, `fill_labels` (with `read_labels`, as the
   write runs it before the checks) and `write_checks.check_before_update(db_instance, the head as read)`; then one
   read transaction: the judge's alternatives looked up (`ENTITIES_QUERY`, kept for its messages: not there,
   deleted, merged away), and, when all are there, `validate_write.check_references_exist` on **the suggestion's own
   part of the plan** (`WritePlan(head=<the suggestion's PlannedNode>)`, found by its id in `plan_update`): every
   reference the replacement adds, its `fields` included, checked as the write checks it (`has_allowed_label`).
   The old `one.type in labels` check is gone (the write's check replaces it); "not of the class it says" now says
   "is not of an allowed type". What the rest of the head names is not checked there (the head's, not the judge's).
   The trial runs under `as_toolchain(toolchain)` as the write does (admin as the write's: see 8).
6. **Whether a before-hook can write**: `check_before_update` itself writes nothing; it runs the project's
   `@before_create`/`@before_update`, whose contract is to read and refuse (`write_checks`' docstring). Nothing in
   Pangloss's packages declares one. A project's hook that wrote would write once more in the trial than in the
   write: outside their contract; said in `reconcile.md`, the hooks kept in the trial.
7. **Found while testing point 4**: a node new to a head that is given an id (the worker's suggestion always is)
   becomes an `UpdateDB` with no previous node, and `check_before_update` runs **neither** its `@before_create` nor
   its `@before_update`, in the write as in the trial: the write writes a value such a hook would refuse. The brief's
   test "a `@before_create` of a `RECONCILE_SUGGEST_AS` subclass" is therefore an `xfail(strict=True)` (the
   behaviour wanted, with the reason); beside it a passing test of a `@before_update` **of the head** that refuses
   the judge's value (put on `Factoid` in the test with `monkeypatch`: checks are looked up on the class when they
   run). Not mended: it is in `pangloss-models` (`write_checks`), outside this brief.
8. **Who the judge runs as** (point 6): `as_toolchain` left `current_request_is_admin` at its default True. It
   gains `admin=None|bool`; the judge is called under `as_toolchain(toolchain, admin=False)` (the toolchain user is
   no admin: `create_toolchain` makes none). **The write is left as it was** (admin True): Pangloss itself reads
   `current_request_is_admin` only for an assignment of a reviewer on a create and for `acknowledge_citations=removals`
   over the API, neither of which the worker's update does, but a project's before-hook may read it, so changing it
   could change what the worker may write; left, and said.
9. **Copies** (point 7): `takes` is worked out in `judged` (the worker's own list) and given to `asked` as a tuple
   (`asked.takes["classes"]`); the answer is checked against the worker's list. The rest of `Asked` was already
   made fresh for each call (deep copies); nothing else in it is read by the worker afterwards. `Judging.asked` is no
   longer async (it reads nothing).
10. **A head's own refusal met while the judge's data is made** (point 5): `Judging.of` and the loop of `judged` are
    in one `try`; a `PanglossWriteError` or `ValidationError` (not one of `NOT_REFUSALS`) that comes out of it (only
    `Judging.takes`'s `to_db_model`, or `Judging.of`, can raise one: `tried` turns its own into `JudgeFailed`) goes to
    the same branch as the update's refusal (`refused`, a local function now shared by both), with the stand-ins the
    worker's own decision leaves noted (untakable + those `decide_for` decides nothing for), as without a judge. The
    loop of `judged` moved into `Worker._judged`, which gives the verdict or the `JudgeFailed`.
11. **`judged`** (point 8): `_suggested_row` keeps `displayed` as the worker decided always (the first `DISPLAYED`
    candidates), and adds `judged: True` only when the judge's own alternatives were written (`verdict.by_judge` of
    a decided stand-in; a `Leave` writes nothing); no key otherwise, so with no judge the record is as it was.
    `decisions`: the record's `judged` is read with it and the example gets `e.judged = true` or null; the earlier
    example read again (`SUGGESTION_EXAMPLE_READ_QUERY`) carries it too; `examples()` returns it. `learning.Decision`
    gains `judged`; `fits.learn` counts `judged` (of the suggestions' examples), in `Learnt.counts()`, in the line
    "N decisions to learn from, M of them of confirmed suggestions (J of a project's judge)", and in the fit
    (`judged`, optional like `suggested`: null in an older fit). No migration (plain properties).
12. **`worker_models(more)`** in `test_reconcile_worker_live.py` now calls `more(library, declared)`, `declared` the
    test's classes so far (a project's suggestion class with an `informant: Person`; a `Deputy(Person)`); `Factoid`
    gains a second `@update_validator`, which refuses an update with a suggestion in it only when its label is
    "Takes no suggestion" (the test of point 5).
13. **"A typology class switched off"** is tested with a class that has what a typology sets,
    `__labels_unmanaged__ = ("Person",)` (`Deputy(Person)`, whose nodes lack the label `Person`): a real typology
    `also=Person` cannot be declared beside the test's models: `PanglossModelError: WeightedAlternative[Person].alternatives
    goes to classes whose relations are named differently ... give each its own field` (found; not mine to mend).
14. Point 1's message, as built: "it raised CancelledError of its own (the worker was not being cancelled)".

### Choices

15. **A**: `judged` catches a `PanglossWriteError`/`ValidationError` (not one of `NOT_REFUSALS`) out of
    `Judging.takes` and gives the worker's own decision unchanged (`Verdict(decision)`), the judge not asked: a
    decision of nothing is then left and noted as tried, and a suggestion goes to the write, which refuses it as with
    no judge (the first round's point-5 test still passes so: `refused`, the stranger noted, Jon not). Logged at
    **debug** only ("is not asked ... its place takes no suggestion"), as nothing is logged there without a judge.
    The first round's branch in `_process` (a refusal out of `Judging.of`/the loop sent to `refused`) is removed:
    nothing else there can raise one (`Judging.of` reads the candidates and dumps the view; `tried` makes its own
    `JudgeFailed`), and keeping it would make a head `refused` with a judge that is `nothing` without. Test
    validator: `Factoid.takes_no_suggestion_of_the_stranger_when_it_says_so` in `worker_models` (label
    "Not the stranger").
16. **B**: `suggesting._apart(work)` = `await asyncio.create_task(work, context=contextvars.copy_context())`, used
    for `Judging.takes` and `Judging.tried`, the copy taken inside `as_toolchain`, so the copy has its user. A
    cancellation of the worker cancels the inner task (awaiting a task). Not the async judge itself (not asked; a
    sync one already runs in a copy). **Correction of the first round's choice 6**: Pangloss's packages do declare
    before-hooks that run in the trial: the library's `Identification.note_if_confirmed` (`@before_update`: notes in
    `confirmed_in_write`, by `ContextVar.set` of a new list, an identification a write confirms or that keeps URIs,
    for review's `on_write`); the criteria's `MatchesCriteria.with_the_shared_criteria` (reads the cited shared
    description, checks, sets the worked-out values on the trial's own node, and `effective.note_read`) and
    `check_the_places` (a check of the `same_one` places against `whole_write()`). Each is read-only for the
    database. **Found, not mended** (in `pangloss-criteria`): `note_read` mutates a dict that the worker's context
    already holds after its first write (`_read_when_saved`), which a copy of the context does not keep apart: the
    trial's entry for the head is the same one the write's own hook puts there again, read only in the write's
    transaction to decide whether to rework the citers (safe twice), and forgotten when the write commits; a head
    tried and then not written keeps the entry until its next write. Said in `reconcile.md`; untested.
17. **C**: in `judging.checked` (pure): a `Suggest` with no `fields` whose alternatives are the decision's by
    `(id, percent)`, **in any order**, gives `Verdict(decision)`: the worker's own decision as it is (its types, its
    order, its reason), `by_judge` false, so no `judged` and **no trial** (it writes exactly what the worker would).
    Compared by id and per cent, as the brief says, not by the type the judge names (the worker's type is written).
    `fields={}` counts as no fields.
18. **D**: `Judging.of` under `as_toolchain(toolchain, admin=False)` in `_process`, `takes` under the same in
    `judged`. **The trial stays as the write runs** (`as_toolchain(toolchain)`, admin left True): I read the brief's
    "(as the judge and the trial)" as "under `as_toolchain`, as they are", since the trial is meant to be the write's
    own checks; a before-hook reading `current_request_is_admin` would otherwise judge the trial apart from the
    write. Test: the who-it-runs-as test wraps `api.answered` and `Judging.takes` and records who they ran as.
19. **E**: the param left the parametrized test; a plain test,
    `test_a_before_create_of_the_suggestion_s_class_is_run_neither_in_the_trial_nor_in_the_write`, asserts that the
    judge's "a guess" is written (both mentions written, no judge failure), its docstring pointing at `TODO.md`.
    `checked_suggestion`'s docstring and `reconcile.md` no longer speak of an `xfail`.
20. Docs: `reconcile.md` (who it runs as, the `Suggest` row, "made apart" with what each package's hook does in a
    trial, the limits, the place that takes no suggestion, `judged`, the tests); the guide (a place your checks
    refuse is not asked of; a `Suggest` the same as the decision; checks run once more, in a copy); the skill's row
    ("in a copy of the context").
