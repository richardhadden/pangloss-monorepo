# 10.5c' report (builder): choices as they were made

Written as the work goes; the summary is at the end.

## Choices

1. **Where it lives.** A new module `judging.py` with no database in it: `Asked`, the answers (`Unchanged`, `Leave`,
   `Suggest`), `BadAnswer`, `Verdict`, `checked` (an answer's shape checked and made a decision), `call` (the judge
   awaited under its timeout, a sync one in a thread), `WORKER_FIELDS`. The worker's side (building what the judge is
   given, the trial before the write, the entities looked up) is in `suggesting.py`, at `judged`, which keeps its four
   positional arguments and takes the head's context as a keyword (`judging=`); the three test wrappers of `judged`
   in `test_reconcile_worker_live.py` take `**options` and pass them on.
2. **The document is given as Python reads it**: `view.model_dump(mode="json", by_alias=False)`, snake_case, as the
   models' fields are named. Everything else in `Asked` is snake_case too (`stood_for`, `held_by`); the candidates
   and what they share carry only `id`, `type`, `label` (and `documents` for what is shared) of their reference views,
   so no key of theirs is camelCase. One rule for a judge: Python's names throughout.
3. **The candidates** are `api.answered(found, see_unpublished=True)` of the head's stand-ins, one call for the head
   (the reference views as the stored route reads them, those the reader cannot see left out, `shared` cut to
   `RECONCILE_REASONS`), the first `candidates.DISPLAYED` of each. Built only when a judge is configured.
4. **`stand_in`**: `id`, `label`, `stood_for`, `uris`, `place` (the field names from the head down, `place_of`) and
   `held_by` (the class of the node whose field holds it: the head, a nested document, a reified relation such as an
   `InRole`).
5. **`takes.classes`**: the classes of entity the place takes, found by planning the trial of the shape (the stand-in
   replaced by a suggestion of a placeholder in the shape found, `to_db_model` and `plan_update` of that `Update`,
   nothing read): the `allowed_types` the planner gives the placeholder's reference, the same list the write checks a
   reference against (subclasses included). Only with a judge configured.
6. **Merged away is a bad entity**: the write would follow `merged_into` to the survivor, but then what is written is
   not what the judge said, and two alternatives could become one; the judge is told the survivor's id instead.
7. **Duplicates**: an entity named twice in `Suggest.alternatives` is a bad answer (the write would make two edges to
   it in one `WeightedAlternative`).
8. **`edge_properties`** is a worker's field too (the worker keeps the relation's own properties from the stand-in):
   naming it in `fields` is a bad answer, beside the seven of the brief.
9. **Ids**: a reference's `id` has to be a UUID (as text or a `UUID`); `type` a non-empty text. A reference is a
   mapping (`{"type", "id"}`), not an object with those attributes.
10. **Order and per cent**: the alternatives sorted by weight, heaviest first (equal weights in the judge's order),
    then `suggest.percentages` of the weights; the one-entity shape takes the first of that order.
11. **Reasons**: `suggest.Reason` gains `"judged"` (a decision the judge made by `Suggest`) and `"left"` (`Leave`).
12. **The digest of "tried"** holds the judge's name when one is configured (`decided_by` gains `"judge"` only then):
    turning a judge on, or changing it, asks again for the stand-ins tried; with no judge the digest is what it was.
13. **`displayed` with a judge**: what was written (the ids of the judge's alternatives) only when the judge replaced
    the decision (`Suggest`); with `None`/`Unchanged` the rows are as without a judge (the list decided on). See the
    end for why this is uncertain.
14. **`toolchain_version`**: `"profile who, judge package.module:function"`, `"profile who, fit <id>, judge ..."`:
    the judge last, for every suggestion of a batch in which one was configured (read once for the batch, in `Seen`).
15. **Failures are logged at warning** on `pangloss.reconcile.worker`, with the head's class and id, the stand-in's id
    and label, the judge's name and what was wrong (`exc_info` when the judge raised).
16. **`takes.classes` is of entities only**: the planner's `allowed_types` of the place also names documents it takes
    (review's `Unreconciled`, the stand-in itself); those are left out (only classes that are `Entity` subclasses).
17. **The trial of a judge's alternatives** (`Judging.tried`): first the class against `takes.classes` (the clearest
    message), then `prepare_rewrite` with that one replacement **and `to_db_model` of it** (a class's
    `@update_validator` runs there, as in the write), then one read of the entities (`ENTITIES_QUERY`: by
    `PGIndexableNode(id)`, `labels`, `deleted`, `merged_into`; the given type has to be among the node's labels).
    `citations.resolve` and `fill_labels` are not run in the trial (they change nothing a judge chose).
18. **A head all of whose mentions failed** writes nothing and completes its mark (outcome `nothing`), as the brief's
    point 4 says; the failed mentions are asked again at the next save or trigger. The code did not make another rule
    more natural: the mark is the head's, and the mention's "not tried" is what brings it back.
19. **The judge's failure is not a head failure by construction**: `judged` raises `JudgeFailed`, caught in the loop
    of `_process`, so nothing reaches `Worker.process`'s count. A failure of the worker's own side while building
    what the judge is given (`Judging.of`: the read of the candidates' views; `takes`) is the worker's, and raises as
    any failure of the head does (counted).
20. **`Stats.judge_failures`** counts the mentions a judge failed for (not asked for by the brief; cheap and used by a
    test).
21. **`worker_models(more=None)`** in `test_reconcile_worker_live.py` takes a function that declares more classes
    before `initialise()` (the subclass `NotedSuggestion` of the test of further fields). The only other change to that
    file: the three wrappers of `judged` pass `**options` on.
22. **The test of statements**: a drain with no setting and a drain with a judge that answers None, on identical
    heads after a first drain (so the toolchain is made and nothing else differs): the statement texts are the same,
    in the same order, once the slice run inside `api.answered` (the candidates' reference views and the states) is
    taken out. With no setting, `Judging.of` and `judging.call` are patched to raise, and the drain writes.
23. **The pure test of an import that fails** names a helper module, `tests/reconcile_judge_raises_on_import.py`,
    which raises on import (the import's own error is in the refusal); the live test checks it is not in
    `sys.modules` after a drain with no setting.
24. **The guide's worked judge** is async, reads each candidate with `Person.get`, and runs the project's sync
    `ask_model(prompt) -> str` in a thread; it answers `Leave()` for "none", `Suggest` of the weights it got, and
    `Unchanged()` when it chose nobody. Not run (it imports a project's models).

## Uncertain

- `displayed` when a judge answers `Unchanged`/`None` stays "the list decided on" (the five), as without a judge;
  only a `Suggest` makes it "what was written". The brief's "displayed what was written" could be read for every
  suggestion of a batch with a judge; that would change what the fit learns from with a judge that changes nothing.
- A sync judge past its timeout keeps its thread (and a slot of the default executor) until it returns.
- An async judge that blocks the loop (a sync call inside it) stops the renewal of the lease: not guarded.
- What the trial cannot see (a uniqueness or a check made only in the write's transaction) can still refuse the whole
  update, as before: then no mention of the head is written.
