## The step

Today `part.refresh_ids` gives every head of a batch to one `maths.refresh`. A batch of 200 factoids is past
`FASTRP_REFRESH_CAP` together though each alone is not, so all of it is made approximately and counted stale. Decided
by the user: on `TooLarge` the batch is halved, each half refreshed by itself, down to one; only what is past the cap
alone is approximated.

## What main found and decided (do not re-derive; build on it)

1. **Why groups stay exact.** `maths.refresh(reader, maps, centre, cap)` returns, for every stop within `k - 1`
   joins of `centre`, the vector a rebuild would give, computed from the database as it is now. The answer for a
   stop does not depend on what else is in `centre`. So for any way of dividing the centre into groups, the union of
   the groups' answers is the answer of the whole. And `changed_stops(reader, graph, heads, before)` is a union over
   its heads and its `before`: the centre of a group of units is the union of each unit's.
2. **The units** of a batch are: each head written, each head removed, and each id of `before` (a stop a written
   head named before; the worker does not say which head named it, and need not). A group is some units; its centre
   is `changed_stops(reader, graph, heads of the group, before-ids of the group)`.
3. **One reader for all the groups, that remembers** (trial, `:7690`, 200 factoids: 249 s without, 53 s with, the
   same vectors). All the attempts run inside one `reading(...)`, on a reader that asks the database for an id at
   most once: `nodes`, `out_edges`, `in_edges`, `citing` and `below`, each cached by id. `citing` and `below` need
   their queries to return which id each row answers (`c.target_id`; the head) so that they can be cached by id.
   Put it in `cypher_reader.py` (a subclass of `CypherReader`, or a flag: your choice), with `seen` kept as now.
   **A bound on its memory**: when it holds more than `10 × cap` nodes it forgets everything (it reads again;
   nothing else changes). Say the number as a named constant.
4. **No remembered size of group** (the trial: the size started from hardly matters once the reader remembers):
   halve from the whole batch. Units in sorted order, the first half `units[: n // 2]`.
5. **What is past the cap alone**:
   - a head written: approximated as today (`approximately`), and its approximate vectors are stale;
   - a head removed, or a `before` id: left as it was, 1 stale each (as a removed head is counted today).
6. **An approximate vector never replaces an exact one of the same turn.** A stop of a head approximated may also
   be within reach of another group, which then has its exact vector. So: the exact rows are written first;
   `approximately` is given the ids that are exact in this turn and neither writes, removes nor counts them. (It
   reads the stored vectors of the neighbours, which by then are the fresh ones.)
7. `Refreshed.written` is the exact rows; `Refreshed.stale` the approximate rows written plus the units of 5's
   second kind. The meaning of both is unchanged.
8. The grouping itself is to be a **pure function** that a test can call with a fake attempt (given the units and a
   function `attempt(group) -> result or None`, it returns the results and the units that failed alone), so that
   "the calls made are the halves expected" is tested without a database.

## Tests

- *Pure*, `tests/test_fastrp_exactness.py` (new cases, in the file's own style and with its oracle): random edits as
  now; the changed centre's units divided into 2, 3 and 7 groups in a shuffled order, each refreshed by itself, the
  union equal to a rebuild to 1e-5; once with the removed heads and the `before` ids all in the first group, once
  all in the last.
- *Pure*, `tests/test_fastrp_part.py`: the grouping with an attempt that is too large above n units: the groups
  tried are the halves expected, in order; a unit too large alone is the only one returned as failed; with
  everything fitting there is exactly one attempt. And the remembering reader over a counting fake of the queries:
  an id asked twice goes to the database once, for each of the five reads; past the bound it forgets and still
  answers rightly.
- *Live*, `tests/live/test_fastrp_worker_live.py` (both backends run it; you run Memgraph): with a small cap (50, or
  what your planted data needs: say which), a batch of 12 factoids written before one turn of the worker, one of
  which names a planted hub that is past the cap alone and the other eleven of which are nowhere near it: after the
  one turn the eleven's stops (and what they name) have the vectors a rebuild gives (the file's existing way of
  comparing with the oracle), the stops of the one are stale, and the stale count of the part's state is the
  number of that one's approximate vectors, not of the batch. Also: a batch with a removed head and an update that
  drops a reference, refreshed in groups (a cap that forces a split), equals a rebuild.
