## What main decided (build these as said)

### 1. Whose vector a date is

- A date **held by a date** (an end of a `DateRange`, the start or end of a `Period` or `Recurrence`) is a part
  of that date: it counts for nothing itself. Only a date that something other than a date holds counts.
- Such a date counts for **what holds it, if that is a document or an entity** (a nested statement with `when`
  has a vector of its own), and for **the head it is under** (a factoid has the dates of all its statements; a
  head that holds the date itself is counted once).
- A date held by anything else (a reified relation, an embedded object, a semantic space) counts for the head
  only.
- A date whose `precision` is `unknown` (an `UnknownDate` by itself) counts for nothing.
- An item's vector is `maths.vector(the bounds of its dates, ring)`: each date at length one, added. An item with
  no date that counts has **no vector**. Only an item of a class that has vectors (`pangloss_vectors.meta.included`)
  gets one, and only one a reader could be shown as the name part says it (`pangloss_names.part._kept`: not merged
  into another, its head there and not deleted). `_kept` and `BELOW_QUERY` are already copied twice in the
  workspace (`TODO.md` says so): do not make a third copy without saying in the report where the three are, so
  that main can decide to move them.

### 2. Settings (`pangloss_dates_vectors/settings.py`), checked at `initialise()` as `NAMES_DIMENSION` is

- `DATES_VECTOR_DIMENSION`: a whole number from 8 to 2048, default **128**.
- `DATES_VECTOR_WIDTH`: years, a number more than 0 and at most 100, default **2** (an `int` or a `float`).
- `ring() -> maths.Ring`. A refusal names the setting and what it may be (`PanglossInitialisationError`).

### 3. The part (`pangloss_dates_vectors/part.py`): `"time"`

`name = "time"`, `local = True`, `weight = 0.0` (in no profile until a project's `VECTORS_PROFILES` weighs it),
`requires` as the name part's.

- `dimension()`: the ring's. `signature()`: the dimension, the width, and the classes that have vectors (so a
  class newly excluded or included is a rebuild due; see what the name and class parts put in theirs and why).
- `rebuild(write)`: every date in pages (the page query below, by `fragments` so it holds on both backends).
  **Do not hold a matrix of every date by the dimension** (a project may have a million dates): keep the rows
  compactly (the four bounds as integers, the items each date counts for), then work in batches of items with
  `maths.of_items` and `write` each batch. Returns the count written.
- `refresh(changed, write)`: for the heads written, the dates under them (the second query below), the vectors of
  the items they count for, written; and **the time vector removed** of any written head, or document under it,
  that has one stored and now has no date that counts (as the name part removes a name vector:
  `store.read_part`, `store.remove_vectors`). Read how `NamePart.refresh` decides which ids to look at
  (`changed.written`, the documents below) and follow it. Nothing is ever stale: a refresh is exact
  (`Refreshed(written=n, stale=0)`).
- `overlay(plan)`: the vectors of the items of the plan (its head and the documents in it) from the dates in the
  plan, by the same rule, writing nothing. The bounds are on the DB models the plan is made of (find how the plan
  holds its nodes and what holds what; `NamePart.overlay` reads only `plan.head.properties`, so this needs more:
  say in the report what you read). The suite's claim is that the overlay equals the same payload written.

### 4. The queries (tried on Memgraph, 27,761 dates: 0.6 s; plans in `dates_query_trial.py`'s output when run)

What holds a date is at the end of its one reverse edge (`(d)-[r]->(n) WHERE r.reverse = true`). A date is a
`:PGIndexableNode` with `head_node_id`; `PGIndexableNode(id)` and `PGIndexableNode(head_node_id)` are indexed.

    MATCH (d:PGIndexableNode) WHERE d.id > $after AND d:Date
    WITH d ORDER BY d.id LIMIT $limit
    OPTIONAL MATCH (d)-[r]->(n) WHERE r.reverse = true
    OPTIONAL MATCH (h:HeadNode {id: d.head_node_id})
    RETURN collect([d.id, <the four bounds>, d.precision, n.id, n.type, n:Date, n:Document OR n:Entity,
                    <the head's id and type>, <kept>]) AS rows

    UNWIND $ids AS id
    MATCH (d:PGIndexableNode {head_node_id: id}) WHERE d:Date
    ... the same

Use `fragments` for the head, deleted and merged as the name part does. Check one thing the trial did not: a date
that a **head** holds itself (an entity's own date, `Person.born` below): is its `head_node_id` the head's id, so
that the second query finds it? If not, the second query needs the dates held by the head too; say what you found.
Page as the name part does (the largest id of the page; a page short of the limit is the last).

  None` (a date that counts for the head only). Nothing changes when `dated` is false: the other parts' tests
  must give the counts they gave before (say them in the report).
- `PartSuite` gains `dated: ClassVar[bool] = False`; every `declare(...)` in `suite.py` goes through one method
  that passes it. When the models are dated, **the payloads of `writes.py` and of `overlay_payload` say dates**
  (so that every claim of the suite, the delete, the merge, the publish, the overlay, the rebuild under writes,
  is made of dated documents): a day, a year, a `DateRange`, a `Period` with an end unknown, two statements of one
  factoid with different dates and one with none, a person born, a participant `during`. One helper decides
  (`m.dated`, which `Names(locals())` already gives), so that the undated payloads stay byte for byte what they
  were.

## Tests

- Pure, `tests/test_time_part.py` (a `conftest.py` as `pangloss-names/tests/conftest.py`): the settings' defaults
  and refusals; the signature changes with the dimension, the width and the classes with vectors and with nothing
  else; the part registers at weight 0 and the default profile has no `time`.
- Live, `tests/live/test_time_part_live.py`, on the backend under test: **the suite called for `"time"` with
  `dated = True`**, its slow way a plain query written in the test (not the part's) and `maths.vector`. And the
  part's own:
  - a nested statement's date gives the statement and its head a vector, and the head's is of all its statements'
    dates;
  - a `DateRange` and a `Period` count once (not their ends as well);
  - a date on a wrapper (`InRole.during`) counts for the head and not for the statement that holds the wrapper;
  - a person born has a vector, and loses it when an update takes the date away; a factoid whose update changes a
    date has the new date's vector after one turn, and one whose update takes every date away has none;
  - an `UnknownDate` alone gives no vector; a `DateRange` with one end unknown gives one;
  - a class excluded (`VECTORS_EXCLUDE`) gets no time vector while its head still does;
  - a refresh before any rebuild: see what the worker does with a part never built and what the name part's test
    of it asserts, and assert the same or say why it differs.
- Run on Memgraph (`:7687`): `uv run pytest packages/pangloss-dates-vectors -q`, then
